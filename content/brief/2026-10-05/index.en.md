## Nvidia-backed Reflection is preparing a US open-weight model for this month

Reflection, a US startup backed by Nvidia, is preparing to release its first open-weight model within October, Axios reported on October 4. The company was founded in 2024 by two former Google DeepMind researchers, and it raised $2 billion at an $8 billion valuation a year ago. Its first model is expected to trail the top US frontier models at launch. It should be competitive with the leading Chinese open-weight systems, and capable enough for a company to layer its own data on top and build a cheaper custom AI. A Reflection spokesperson declined to comment. [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/04/reflection-open-weight-ai) · [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/scoop-powerful-model-startup-reflection-124931934.html)

Reflection is not alone. Other Western open-weight models ship this month too, according to Axios. The strongest open-weight systems today are almost all Chinese; the big US labs ship closed models only. That gap is the opening. Nvidia and Reflection want to pair GPUs with open weights, so a company keeps its own data. Reflection has briefed Washington officials in recent weeks on the release and on its longer-term "AI factory" plan, a vision Jensen Huang has pushed for years. [The News](https://www.thenews.com.pk/latest/1418712-new-open-ai-model-could-change-the-global-ai-race-heres-how)

## A downloadable alternative to the closed APIs is arriving

Until now, near-frontier quality meant the Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google API. A US open-weight model adds a real choice: download the weights, run them yourself, fine-tune on your own data, and leave when a vendor raises prices. Axios reported that combining a cheap open model with good company data can match expensive frontier systems in some situations. Nothing here says to migrate the product you already run. It does add a question to stack decisions: can I run this myself later?

Nothing is confirmed yet. Reflection declined to comment. The model's performance, license, and price are all unpublished.

- Worth doing now: keep prompts and eval sets separate from any single model, so a new open-weight release takes an afternoon to A/B rather than a rewrite.
- Worth watching: the license terms (commercial use, fine-tuning) and the real hardware requirements of this month's Western open-weight releases.
