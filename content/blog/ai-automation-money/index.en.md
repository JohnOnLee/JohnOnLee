Open YouTube to learn automation and you get n8n, Make, Zapier. The enterprise-market tools from [the last piece](/en/blog/ai-automation-two-markets/) (Boomi, Workato, UiPath) are almost nowhere. The job ads clearly want them; the content layer barely mentions them.

The easy answer is views: beginners are the audience, so beginner tools win. That's half of it; the other half is where the money comes from. Follow who pays the teacher.

## The first money: affiliate commissions

n8n runs [an affiliate program for creators](https://n8n.io/affiliates/): 30% of referred cloud-subscription revenue, for twelve months. Every viewer who signs up through a creator's link becomes recurring income for that creator, and Make runs [a similar program](https://www.make.com/en/affiliate). Zapier [has no affiliate program open to ordinary creators](https://community.zapier.com/show-tell-5/does-zapier-have-an-affiliate-program-13950); it holds the same shelf space through search dominance instead.

Enterprise vendors don't pay creators this kind of commission. There is no per-signup commission open to individuals. [Workato](https://www.workato.com/partners) and [UiPath](https://www.uipath.com/partners/become-a-partner) do run partner revenue programs, but the money arrives after a partner agreement and a closed enterprise deal. A video can't collect on that, so a YouTuber teaching Workato has effectively nobody to collect from.

Which means a creator's menu was set before the first video went up.

## The second money: the community business

The monetization point in automation education now sits below the videos, on Skool, a community platform. The two biggest automation communities there, [AI Automation Agency Hub](https://www.skool.com/learn-ai/about) (330,000+ members) and [AI Automation Society](https://www.skool.com/ai-automation-society/about) (440,000+), are both free.

The free tier brings people in, and what's sold behind it differs by operator. AI Automation Society sells [a $129-a-month membership](https://www.skool.com/ai-automation-society-plus/about) to its 440,000 free members; the AAA Hub side sells an accelerator priced above $5,000 to its 330,000 (pricing disclosed only on a sales call; that figure comes from student accounts). Where the published numbers allow an estimate, at AI Automation Society, the paid share sits under 1%: 447,000 free vs 3,700 paid is 0.8%. Strictly a paid-to-free count taken on the same day, not a conversion rate.

Skool adds a competition on top. [Skool Games](https://help.skool.com/article/182-skool-games-leaderboard-faq) ranks community owners each quarter by the new subscription revenue they add in 90 days, and the leaderboard counts at most $100 a month per subscription. The contest is built for low-priced volume, and the curriculum that sells at volume is a low-barrier tool. The answer, again, is n8n. High-priced accelerators aren't subscriptions, so the leaderboard doesn't count them at all.

## The other side is nearly free, and invisible

Enterprise education is much bigger and barely shows up on YouTube. UiPath Academy reported [1.6 million learners](https://ir.uipath.com/news/detail/311/introducing-the-next-evolution-of-uipath-academy-a-gateway-to-ai-at-work) (UiPath, 2023). Salesforce Trailhead issued [2.6 million+ AI and data badges](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2024/09/18/ai-training-opportunities/) in the fifteen months from June 2023, about 40% of them going to Salesforce's own employees. [Workato's courses and exams cost nothing](https://www.workato.com/certification).

Workato aside, what's free is the training, not the credential. [UiPath's official exams run $150–300](https://www.uipath.com/learning/certification/faqs), and [Salesforce's free first attempt at its AI certifications](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/salesforce-candidate-ai-certification/) ended at the close of 2025. The credential track itself is churning under the AI transition: Microsoft [retired its Power Automate certification](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/retired-certification-exams) this year and replaced it with an agent certification.

Creators teaching the enterprise tools do exist. There are channels devoted to Power Automate, and the Salesforce world supports a paid-course market of its own. But since no vendor pays per signup, those creators have to earn outside the vendors, at a much smaller scale. Which leaves learners looking at an inverted world: the education that costs money is the most visible, and the education that's nearly free is the hardest to find.

## This is not a scam story

Some things deserve saying in the other direction. The big accelerator's review score is solid: [4.7 on Trustpilot across 548 reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/aaaaccelerator.com), though the account actively invites customers to review, so read it with that in mind. A different community backs itself with a full-refund 90-day guarantee tied to landing your first client (Maker School, $184 a month). [Freelance-market guides](https://ciphernutz.com/blog/hire-n8n-expert-cost-pricing-guide) put n8n rates at $40–100 an hour, $125–250 senior (agency tallies, not marketplace statistics). Real people earn real money in this market.

The problem isn't fraud. It's distortion: several hundred thousand people are being trained toward the smallest part of the market companies actually pay for, and the direction was set by the teachers' revenue structure rather than the learners' needs.

## How to use the content layer anyway

Learning n8n is fine in itself — the last piece showed real jobs on it and real enterprises adopting it. Three things to keep.

- Check whether a channel teaches only the tool its affiliate links point at. A curriculum with one tool as every answer is a funnel, not a curriculum.
- Before paying for a course, sweep the free tier: vendor academy training costs nothing, enterprise ads recognise vendor certifications, and a $150–300 exam is cheaper than most paid courses.
- Spend what's left, money and hours, on records. Whoever's hiring reads a workflow with an error rate and throughput attached before any certificate. That was the last piece's closing table.
